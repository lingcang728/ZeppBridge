## 1. Raycast — shortcut-first utility, high-energy surface

## Comparative findings

### Navigation and page destinations

The most useful pattern is a small set of navigation responsibilities that correspond to visitor questions: *what it does, where it runs, how it works, and where to get help*. Things and Day One keep that structure clear; Alfred and Screen Studio expose a legitimate learning/help path; Raycast and CleanShot use broader product groupings that make sense for suites with many feature pages. Avoid adopting their breadth without comparable scope. This research verified reversible detail-page navigation for Things, Bear, Day One, Fantastical, Alfred, Ulysses and Screen Studio. Raycast AI and Craft Learn were reachable as official pages directly, but homepage click did not confirm the route. CleanShot’s Cloud route timed out, while the recording route was exposed from Product. Those distinctions matter: URL presence is not proof the intended homepage path works.

Mobile nav behavior ranged from still-visible links (Things, Alfred) to a full-screen drawer (Day One, Screen Studio), while Raycast uses a side sheet. Day One required a second toggle press after Escape; Screen Studio closed on Escape. Fantastical had a measurable 38px overflow and no visible hamburger in this run. CleanShot’s menu trigger did not confirm opening; Bear’s HTML drawer had no exposed state attribute. One Tab per page found visible focus for Raycast, Day One, Alfred and several logo/nav links, but this is only a spot check, not an accessibility audit. The menu evidence does not justify asserting keyboard completeness.

### Typography and layout rhythm

The reference set covers compact system typography (Things), expressive display serif (Craft, Alfred), clean sans hero scale (Raycast, Bear, Day One, Ulysses), and oversized modern sans with tight tracking (CleanShot). One apparent pattern is that even a large headline is paired with a short explanatory line and a real visual artifact; the visitor never has to infer what the product looks like. Things and Bear make this especially plain. Craft’s contrasting serif title and workspace sample yield a more editorial feel. Raycast and CleanShot use headline scale as part of a performance/tool aesthetic. These identities are references for hierarchy, not fonts or colors to transplant.

Several selector caveats surfaced: Things’ H1 is semantically present but visually 1×1px; Ulysses’ first H1 is a later dark-section title, not the visible hero. Therefore screenshot observation, computed CSS, and semantic structure should be reported separately where they disagree. No formal color-contrast ratio was computed; high contrast is a visual observation only.

### Motion and initial-load behavior

Motion should be considered as product evidence and as a potential obstacle. Raycast’s animated glint/ribbon, Fantastical’s playing hero loop, CleanShot’s playing demo/carousel, and Ulysses’ slow particles all changed over time in the screenshots; pixel-difference values are rough frame comparisons at 1.6s, not a usability metric. Things’ intro video was paused. Bear’s autoplay-declared videos were paused. Screen Studio’s main demo was paused, with other embeds in mixed states. Craft accordions and several sites’ UI elements report CSS transitions or animations even when no meaningful hero movement was visible. A `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` context was set; Raycast, Craft, Fantastical, Ulysses and CleanShot still exposed animation names or visible change in the sampled state. This limited test cannot prove whether all animation was reduced or can be paused. ZeppBridge should check its own motion state under the preference explicitly, keep essential content in a static first frame, and provide visible pause controls for any automatically moving sample.

Product-level design recognition was verified via Apple’s official award history for Things and Bear, Apple’s App Store editorial item for Craft, MacRumors’ 2014 summary for Day One, Apple Developer’s Fantastical feature, official Alfred and Ulysses information, and MacStories’ 2024 app roundup for Raycast. These support product-level relevance, not the excellence of the current marketing pages. Screen Studio and the older CleanShot page have third-party editorial notes from One Page Love, useful specifically for presentation patterns. No claims about web-design awards, revenue, popularity, or endorsement are inferred from these references.

## Three strongest references for ZeppBridge

1. **Things** — strongest visual reference for a calm, product-led archive. It gives one short product identity and follows with a clear application screen, then lets a few purposeful sections explain how it works. Transfer the restrained surface, whitespace and real UI proof while preserving ZeppBridge’s distinctive existing palette and labels.
2. **Screen Studio** — strongest interaction/presentation reference. Its demo illustrates capability before a visitor installs anything, and the feature page explains a concrete workflow. ## Principles to apply to the ZeppBridge page

1. Lead with one plain sentence about keeping and exploring the user’s Zepp health archive, then show the real sample app immediately. Do not begin with an abstract dashboard illustration.
2. Pair every feature promise with its visible proof or a direct detail destination. Explain imported records, timeline, search/export and connection methods using the actual product state.
3. Keep global navigation short: product/demo, privacy/data flow, connection or download, and help/source. Capability breadth can live lower on the page or on detail routes.
4. Make local ownership concrete with a simple, truthful data-flow diagram: source, local archive, optional export/handoff. State what leaves the machine and only when. Maintain `null`/missing semantics rather than implying missing measurements are zero.
5. Show platform support in one compact, real selection pattern. Check that mobile layout has no overflow and important routes remain reachable at 390px.
6. Preserve a static and informative first render. Any sample interaction should be opt-in, keyboard-operable and reversible; do not require autoplay to reveal the evidence.
7. Retain the current warm-paper/olive/charcoal direction and MiSans for UI. Borrow layout discipline and proof placement rather than another product’s palette, brand graphics or sales voice.

## Patterns to reject

- Fantastical’s observed mobile horizontal overflow, because it clips the page beyond the phone viewport.
- Feature clicks that look like navigation but do not produce a confirmed destination; avoid routing ambiguity and provide direct usable detail paths.
- Reliance on an autoplay hero video, slow carousel or moving background as the only explanation of function. The static state must still show the real product and purpose.
- Large third-party platform/store badges without explaining availability or the route they open.
- Borrowed user counts, testimonials, health claims, claims about medical benefit, implied adoption, or privacy language that exceeds ZeppBridge’s actual behavior.
- A mobile menu that removes useful top-level routes without an accessible toggle, visible focus, Escape/close behavior and understandable state.
- Treating product awards or an editorial page feature as a web-design award or evidence that an unrelated product will have similar results.

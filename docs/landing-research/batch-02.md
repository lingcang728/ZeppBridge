## Per-site observations

### OpenArt

**Observed, rendered:** a black canvas, green offer strip, immersive video stills around the headline “Where ideas become visual stories,” and a magenta “Start Creating Now” action (“It’s free”). Header groups AI Tools, AI Models, Features, Resources, Pricing and Enterprise, with a second “Start for Free” action. ## Cross-site principles for ZeppBridge

1. State the product’s job before its technology. Strong examples pair a clear user outcome with a specific object or workflow; ZeppBridge should lead with local Amazfit/Zepp health-data history, not AI.
2. Show the actual interface near the promise. Figma, Notion and Otter use product context to make capability legible; ZeppBridge’s sample-data app can demonstrate real charts and records without invented outcomes.
3. Give the first screen one main route and one useful alternative. Otter’s start/demo split and OpenRouter’s API/model split are explicit. For ZeppBridge, make Download and Explore sample data the visible choices.
4. Make privacy and data flow concrete. OpenRouter’s data-policy card and OpenEvidence’s eligibility framing show how a concern can be surfaced beside the product. ZeppBridge should explain local storage and optional deliberate AI handoff with a plain diagram and truthful copy.
5. Organize deeper information by visitor question. OpenRouter’s model catalogue and Notion’s distinct AI/meeting/search pages work as separate reading destinations. ZeppBridge can link Connect, Privacy, Supported data and Downloads from the homepage, while keeping their detail pages focused.
6. Use motion to clarify state, not to manufacture urgency. OpenArt, Notion and Speechify use marquees or autoplay media; the observed animations add page activity but are costly and do not improve archive trust. Keep ZeppBridge’s existing transitions subtle and respect reduced motion.
7. Do not rely on popularity, customer logos or superlatives where evidence is unavailable. Several pages display prominent claims and logo strips. ZeppBridge should use no invented user counts, medical outcomes or third-party endorsements.

**Three strongest references:** Figma’s homepage-to-process explanation is a strong model for pairing real product screens with a legible sequence; Otter’s split hero and two clear actions help separate self-serve exploration from a secondary path; OpenRouter’s Models/Docs/Benchmarks taxonomy shows how to make technical detail discoverable without turning it into the first-screen pitch.

**Patterns to reject:** full-viewport AI spectacle that pushes the product below the fold; auto-rotating or autoplay media as a substitute for evidence; endless marquees, offer ribbons and layered signup/cookie modals; mega-menus that expose every internal capability; partner logos, testimonials or numerical proof without permission and verification.

**Concrete transfer suggestions:** keep the current five-beat story and interactive sample as the visual center; let the first screen pair “local health-data archive” with a real sample view and buttons for Download and Explore sample; use a compact navigation set such as Product, Data sources, Privacy, Downloads and Docs; put the data-flow/missing-sample rule beside the demo, making clear that absent samples remain missing; send feature links to focused pages for connection methods, privacy and supported records, without adding account gates or built-in AI promises.

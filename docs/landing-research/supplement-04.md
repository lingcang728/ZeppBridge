# Supplement 04 — software and web-design references

**Captured 2026-10-07.** Ten distinct, non-duplicate brands from the reserved candidate list: Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Framer, Webflow, Readymag, Rive, Spline, Resend, and Sanity. All ten official homepages returned HTTP 200 and rendered in an independent headless Chromium session. The rendered review used 1440×1000 desktop and 390×844 mobile viewports, en-US, America/Los_Angeles, and a fresh unauthenticated context. Each site was inspected at its first screen, at least two lower sections, and one actual feature/use-case destination. We did not log in, submit forms, send messages, purchase, upload, or enter a private product flow.

Screenshots, HTML, computed styles, interaction observations, and motion frame pairs are retained under `C:\Users\15pro\AppData\Local\Temp\zeppbridge-landing-supplement-04\` in one directory per brand. Files include desktop top/scroll, mobile top, measured JSON and (where captured) motion pairs; per-site detail and menu screenshots are also present. Readymag's font readiness timed out during one capture, so its hero typography is reported unknown rather than guessed. Browser review was live, not a local copy. A Jina Reader route failed its TLS handshake in this environment; research fallback used the browser and official URLs plus web search. No TLS verification bypass or software installation was used.

## Site observations

### Linear

**Observed.** The dark, nearly monochrome homepage starts with “The product development system for teams and agents,” a large product workspace image, and a restrained set of actions. The measured desktop display style was Inter Variable/SF Pro Display fallback, 64px at weight 510; mobile measured 38px. The page spaces a product promise, app proof, then the “new species” and intake/integration explanations rather than turning every feature into a colored card. The Product and Resources groups open multi-column menus with clear product/resource responsibilities; the menu collapses on a second activation. Tab focus on About has a distinct indigo outline. `/customers` connects the system promise to named customer stories. Those are customer-published/provided stories and metrics, not proof ZeppBridge should invent comparable claims. A roughly 1.8-second screenshot pair did not visibly change; CSS transition declarations alone are not evidence of motion.

**Transfer.** Borrow the short navigation taxonomy and generous breathing room around one real app view. Use adjacent concise copy to state what the product view proves. Keep the warm paper/olive identity and MiSans; do not adopt Linear's dark palette, developer-team positioning, or agent language. The official [brand guidance](https://linear.app/brand) says the identity should work on light and dark grounds and use ample space; the useful transferable principle is consistency and breathing room, not their exact art direction.

### Stripe

**Observed.** Stripe opens with a financial-infrastructure promise, a light 48px Sohne Variable headline at weight 300 on desktop (34px mobile), and an animated gradient/ribbon visual. The product catalog groups payments, billing, revenue, finance, connect, and security; scrolling shifts from adoption/optimization into concrete capabilities. The Payments page restates the promise in a specific context (“Unified, global payments…”) and decomposes it into conversion and reach. Its layered, animated gradient visibly changed between frames about 1.8 seconds apart (measured image difference 7.39%); the visual is atmospheric rather than a status signal. A 3px violet keyboard focus ring was visible on Get started. Mobile navigation opened and Escape closed it. The homepage contains extensive company scale and customer-performance claims; none are transferable as design evidence.

**Transfer.** The relevant pattern is a clear route from a broad promise to a specific capability page that answers “what does this do?” with short subheadings. ZeppBridge can do this with concrete sections such as sync, missing samples, privacy, and downloads, with each section explaining the benefit and boundary. Avoid the sprawling payments taxonomy, animated gradient, enterprise scale cues, and numbers.

### Vercel

**Observed.** A large, open white field uses Geist Sans, measured at 64px/400 with tight -3.84px tracking on desktop; mobile is 48px/400. A simple geometric mark, short promise (“Agentic Infrastructure”), primary action, and a quieter audience prompt sit ahead of customer logos. Product navigation groups Agent Stack, Core Platform, and Tools; Resources is separately organized. The Observability detail page leads with a production-understanding promise and actual graph/diagnostic examples, making the feature explanation tangible. The homepage screenshot pair showed no visible change over approximately 1.8 seconds. A menu closes on Escape, and keyboard movement reaches the menu controls. The current brand copy heavily foregrounds agents, which is not a fit for ZeppBridge.

**Transfer.** Keep the hero's hierarchy legible by using one dominant promise and one real proof surface, then label secondary routes by visitor intent. Use a real ZeppBridge sample app as proof, with a clear “example data” label. Do not adopt the agent-first story, the extensive infrastructure catalog, or Vercel's stark white/black identity. Awwwards recognition found was for the separate Vercel Ship event site, not this homepage.

### Framer

**Observed.** A black homepage uses GT Walsheim Medium, about 54px/500 desktop and 36px mobile, with a large video/product workspace underneath two direct actions. Platform mega-menu groups create/design/build and related AI/hosting concepts. The `/agents/` destination shows real editing tasks such as updating a site, styling it, and migrating from another tool, with action examples and video; it reconnects through an actual “work alongside you” proposition. The product video/interface changed visibly in a 1.8-second frame pair (21.87% image difference). That establishes visible video movement, not an exact animation duration or reduced-motion fallback. Escape closes the nav menu. The video/AI-led presentation is a poor transfer to a local health archive.

**Transfer.** The useful lesson is to show a product capability doing a specific task and label the task in ordinary language. Apply it to a real, synthetic-data scenario in ZeppBridge—e.g., show a missing sample as missing—while preserving the current real-app demo. Avoid full-bleed looping video, black framing, and AI-centric positioning. Awwwards' Framer 2023 Year in Review reference is an older, separate campaign.

### Webflow

**Observed.** The homepage pairs a vivid blue announcement band and a large, high-contrast headline (“Build for what’s next”) with a large changing product/card rail. Webflow Visual Sans Variable measured 80px/600 desktop and about 46px/600 mobile. Platform's open mega menu divides the product into Build, Manage, Convert, and Extend, with feature links and overview cards; it closes on Escape. Lower sections move from positioning and logos to production tasks like building and publishing. `/feature/design` supports the creativity promise with editable interface imagery and design-specific capabilities. A small amount of card/video movement was visible in the frame pair (3.09% difference in ~1.8s); exact timing and a reduced-motion static state were not established. On mobile, the hamburger and a consent overlay coexist with the first screen. The homepage prominently claims customer outcomes and ROI; do not emulate those without evidence.

**Transfer.** Map only a few real user jobs to information architecture, then give each one a concise explanation and real example. For ZeppBridge, organizing around “connect,” “view,” “understand missingness,” “export,” and “download” would align with actual tasks. Do not import a corporate platform taxonomy, announcement bar, customer proof, or performance claims.

### Readymag

**Observed.** This is an editorial, collage-like design-tool homepage with the short proposition “Make the web more like you” and visible sample compositions. The first view has a centered “Try Readymag” prompt and OneTrust consent UI that cover part of the page. The home headline was not exposed as a semantic H1 in the captured DOM and the font wait timed out, so its hero font and computed headline scale are **unknown**. Do not take the small system-font style from the examples page as the homepage type. Navigation includes Solutions, Pricing, Examples, Templates, Learn, and account actions. Solutions opens a category dropdown (freelancers, agencies, students, teachers, companies); Escape did not dismiss it, but clicking Solutions toggled it closed. The actual Editorial examples page has a clear gallery-led heading and cards that show how editorial work can feel. Mobile was inspected, but the same overlays reduce how much clean first-screen content can be judged.

**Transfer.** The site proves that examples can communicate creative possibility more quickly than abstract adjectives. ZeppBridge can show one authentic, clearly synthetic-data view and one exported report, but must keep the proof quiet and readable. The non-escaping menu is a useful keyboard regression case; the overlays also show why overlays must not conceal the landing-page purpose. The dated Awwwards Honorable Mention relates to a previous 2022 main page only.

### Rive

**Observed.** The first screen is a dark, neon-accented interactive-software showcase with an uppercase “THE INTERACTIVE EXPERIENCE ENGINE” title; Orbitron measured 40px/900 desktop and 21px/900 mobile. Its visual navigation labels Products, Community, Learn, Pricing, Downloads, and Docs are highly stylized; hover/click inspection did not reveal a dropdown, so none is claimed. A left-side category rail (Auto, Product UI, Game UI, Mobile Apps, Film+TV, Websites, among others) frames the demo. Scrolling moves to “Design, code, and animate” and “Build once, ship anywhere,” then a website use-case page demonstrates the tool in context. The rotating showcase visibly changed in the 1.8-second pair (11.94% image difference), though exact timing and reduced-motion behavior were not verified. **Transfer.** Reuse the clarity of a category-specific proof example, especially for how a feature appears in use. For ZeppBridge, put the “missing stays missing” example beside the explanation instead of requiring a visitor to infer it from chart screenshots. Reject the neon game-tool styling, uppercase tech voice, and code-install framing.

### Spline

**Observed.** A dark page opens with “Make anything 3D,” a colorful model/robot composition, and a prompt-like UI. Spline Sans measured 38.4px/500 desktop and 32px/500 mobile. Products hover reveals a set spanning 3D Design, Hana, Omma, AI 3D Generation, embeds, app generation, and export/delivery; clicking/toggling reverses the menu. The `/3d-design` detail route explains interactive production-ready work through modeling, materials, animation, and realtime-data examples. Subtle object movement is visible across the frame pair (~0.48% changed pixels); this is a visual difference, not evidence of a specific duration. No independent award was confirmed.

**Transfer.** A single polished artifact can make a product's nature instantly clear, and the detailed destination should unpack how it is made. ZeppBridge has a stronger fit in a real sample-data app than in decorative 3D: show a local archive screen with one actual data edge case and explain it. Avoid the prompt-shaped CTA, 3D spectacle, and any implication that the health archive is an AI experience.

### Resend

**Observed.** A dark, restrained developer page makes “Email for developers” the main promise. Domaine serif is about 96px/400 desktop and 64px/400 mobile; a black cube gives the hero a quiet focal point. Below are concise CTAs and a code example with SDK/framework tabs. Selecting Next.js changed the displayed source; the page also renders an HTTP 200 example response, which is a visual demo and was not triggered as a real send. On the feature route, “Email API that just works” expands into API calls, scheduling, batching, attachments, and idempotency. A 2px pale-cyan outline was seen on keyboard focus within the code demo. The cube visibly changed across a ~1.8-second frame pair (3.46%); no measured duration or reduced-motion fallback was established. The navigation has Features/Company/Enterprise/Help/Docs/AI/Pricing and account actions; clicking Features did not produce a visible dropdown. On mobile the menu opens and Escape closes it.

**Transfer.** Resend's best move is a proof control that changes the example without pretending to contact a service. ZeppBridge could show connection-path details or platform choices with a reversible control, while preserving keyboard state and exposing text equivalents. Do not copy the dark coder persona, cube animation, large serif, code-first UI, or imply cloud data transfer.

### Sanity

**Observed.** The homepage begins with a 112px/400 Waldenburg heading, 60px on mobile, over a high-contrast photographic/video hero and neon-orange action. The heading reads “The Content Operations Platform.” Products, Solutions, Resources, Docs, Enterprise, and Pricing separate product, audience, and learning content. The product menu exposes Studio, Content Agent, App SDK, and media-library topics, though a consent UI double overlay makes the open visual state difficult to judge from the screenshot. `/studio` gives the product promise a concrete CMS/editor destination. Scrolling moves to a “Mirror how your content operations team works” section, product UI, and numeric outcomes. The ~1.8-second image pair changed 3.84%, mostly in hero/demo visuals; CSS animation counts are not proof of a reduced-motion experience. Consent overlays also intercepted the first keyboard focus, which landed on a privacy control, so nav focus order is not claimed. The current site promotes agent/AI workflows; those are not appropriate as ZeppBridge's core promise.

**Transfer.** Translate product detail into the actual workflow a visitor recognizes: connect a source, review an archive, export intentionally. Keep the visual proof and route to a genuine, narrow detail page. Reject generic content-platform jargon, oversized title/video treatment, consent obstruction, and outcome metrics unless ZeppBridge has a sourced, user-benefiting reason to present them.

## Cross-site synthesis for ZeppBridge

1. **Keep the promise stable while evidence changes.** Linear, Vercel, and Stripe put the main proposition first and let supporting product imagery/details change below it. For ZeppBridge, the headline should remain visible while visitors choose a real feature example; scrolling should not be required to reveal what the product is.
2. **A detail page should cash the homepage's promise.** Stripe's payments page and Vercel's Observability page explain one capability in specific language; Framer, Webflow, Spline, and Sanity show a path from brand promise to real product capability. ZeppBridge should link directly to a concise section/page for sync, privacy, missingness, connections, and downloads, rather than expanding the top navigation into a broad catalog.
3. **Use real product evidence with a label.** Framer's tasks, Resend's selectable code example, and the gallery sites demonstrate through examples. ZeppBridge's real sample app is more credible than a fabricated app mockup. Label its data synthetic, the demo development channel, and the public download stable channel.
4. **Treat motion as decoration unless it communicates state.** Animated gradients, product videos, rotating showcases, and floating 3D objects are visually legible, but add no health-data meaning. The frame pairs establish that some elements move, not that they are accessible or performant. ZeppBridge should keep motion modest, pause it when offscreen, and honor reduced motion; essential information remains available without movement.
5. **Menus need keyboard closure and meaningful focus.** Eight sites had inspectable menu/code paths; Readymag's Solutions menu stayed open after Escape and Sanity's consent overlay obscured navigation focus. Use short direct section links, visible focus, Escape closure for menus, and verify mobile access. This supports the existing brief's choice to avoid a complex mega-menu.
6. **Prefer concrete section labels over corporate categories.** Webflow's Build/Manage/Convert and Rive's category rail are legible within large product suites; ZeppBridge needs only user tasks such as Connect, Browse, Missing data, Export, and Download. Avoid categories visitors must decode.
7. **Proof and claims are separate.** Customer logos, adoption metrics, ROI, and testimonials are prominent on several sites, but they do not prove design quality or product relevance. ZeppBridge should demonstrate its real behavior and explicit missingness contract, without invented health outcomes or borrowed scale claims.

### Three strongest references

- **Linear (editorial rhythm and compact nav):** its short menu responsibilities and open space let a product workspace carry the proof. Transfer as calmer spacing around the actual ZeppBridge app and a small set of direct destinations. The official [brand guidance](https://linear.app/brand) supports space and light/dark flexibility; it is a brand reference, not an award.
- **Resend (reversible proof control):** the framework tab swaps the sample source in place and displays a contained illustrative result. Transfer the reversible interaction model to connection or export examples, with honest local-only copy and keyboard-visible state. Do not transfer its developer aesthetic or demo network semantics.
- **Vercel Observability (specific detail):** the detail page takes a broad promise into an actual graph-and-diagnostic example. Transfer the evidence pattern, not the monochrome visual identity: show one real ZeppBridge behavior and explain its source and limit in nearby text.

### Patterns to reject

- Full-screen background video, auto-changing hero copy, looping spectacle, cursor-follow/scroll hijacking, and motion that is not needed to explain state.
- Enterprise mega-menus, customer logos, adoption/ROI metrics, “AI-first” framing, and server/cloud implications that would obscure local ownership.
- Hero artwork or simulated interfaces presented as if they are the desktop product. Keep the real sample application and synthetic-data boundary explicit.
- Cookie/consent or promotional overlays that obscure the first-screen purpose; keyboard menus that cannot close with Escape.
- Decorative copy that replaces precise health-data boundaries. Missing samples must remain explicitly missing; no zero-fills, inferred rest days, medical outcomes, or remote-health claims.

The strongest direction is already aligned with the local audit: fixed, concise promise; real app proof in the first view; mobile title and actions before the scaled interface; directly reachable feature destinations; calm section rhythm; and reversible, keyboard-accessible controls. This pass provides no reason to add video/3D spectacle, AI positioning, a large nav catalog, or design-award badges. The local audit remains authoritative for actual landing-page defects and baseline accessibility requirements.

## Evidence limits

This is a one-date marketing-site observation, not a usability study or audit of product internals. Geography and CDN response were not varied. Fresh anonymous browser views may omit personalized or authenticated content. Readymag had a font-readiness timeout and overlays; Sanity's consent overlays complicated menu/focus review. Linear and Vercel had no captured visible motion in the sampled interval. Frame-pair percentages are raw visual differences over roughly 1.8 seconds; they do not identify the implementation or duration. Reduced-motion was emulated and computed CSS was recorded, but a complete before/after fallback comparison was not captured, so no site is credited with a verified fallback here. No award is assigned to a site's main homepage unless the cited page specifically covers it; for four brands the reference is a separate campaign or program, and for Rive, Spline, Resend, and Sanity no independent recognition was verified.

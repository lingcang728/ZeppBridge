## Cross-site principles for ZeppBridge

1. **Name the user’s job, then show its next step.** Photomath’s Scan / Solve / Learn sequence and remove.bg’s immediate upload panel make the primary interaction legible without a feature glossary.
2. **Put real product evidence near the promise.** Photomath’s handset, Quark’s desktop-browser mockup, Google AI Studio’s example canvases, and Kimi’s live composer show what a visitor can actually do; use a genuine, synthetic sample archive rather than stock imagery.
3. **Use sections to explain a small workflow.** A few ordered stages can explain connect → keep a local archive → explore/export. Separate those pages from device support and privacy detail, then link them where the question appears.
4. **Group navigation by responsibility.** Docs, downloads, connection support, product explanation and optional AI handoff are distinct user intents. Group them once; do not imitate the creative suites’ model/tool catalogues.
5. **Keep setup choices honest and visible.** Quark’s clear Windows action and the store listings’ explicit product identity show the value of naming the actual platform or publisher. State supported connection paths and what stays on the user’s machine in plain language.
6. **Show gaps without filling them in.** A sample chart or screenshot should preserve missing values as missing. The reviewed sites rarely face this health-data contract; do not borrow any implication that estimates or zeroes are acceptable.
7. **Treat trust copy as evidence, not decoration.** remove.bg’s migration notice is specific and dated; for ZeppBridge, privacy/data-flow copy should likewise describe actual behavior, including the deliberate optional nature of AI handoff.

### Three strongest references

- **Photomath:** best structural reference for turning a complex result into three understandable actions, with readable plain-language explanation beside the product screen.
- **Google AI Studio:** strong reference for showing realistic product output next to a direct action, then separating documentation and pricing from the demo.
- **Quark:** useful reference for a desktop-first download choice, a real software mockup and task-named feature sections. Keep its capability menu much smaller.

### Patterns to reject and transfer

Reject Magnific’s very broad tool taxonomy, Quark’s catalogue breadth, and Emochi’s dense card wall for a calm archive utility. Avoid ambiguous “AI does everything” language, unverified testimonial or metric rows, a required account before visitors can see what the software does, and screenshots that hide the underlying data contract.

Transfer a small, specific story: show one genuine archive example; explain how the user connects a supported Zepp source; show that records stay local and unobserved samples remain absent; then place platform downloads and the optional AI handoff in clearly separated paths. Use actual app screens and concise copy, and put detailed device/privacy guidance one click away without growing the top menu.

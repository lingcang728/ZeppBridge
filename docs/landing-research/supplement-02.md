## Method and evidence

The homepage viewports were 1440 × 960 and 390 × 844 at device scale factor 1; the browser locale was `en-US`. The observed locale is not evidence of the network’s physical region, which I did not determine. All ten homepages returned HTTP 200. I also rendered one official product, feature, help, documentation, security, or transparency destination for each brand. No account or sign-in flow was entered. I did not install tools or touch foreground browser tabs.

For each homepage I saved desktop and mobile top/full-page screenshots, two desktop scroll screenshots, HTML, and a computed-style/navigation/animation snapshot. ## Site findings

### 1. Proton — privacy presented as a connected set of useful tasks

The first fold is very light and centered: a purple serif promise, two short lines of explanation, **Create a free account** and **Compare plans**, then a grid of service marks and trust material. The promise measured 60 px / 63.6 px, weight 400, in ProtonSerif on desktop, stepping to 36 px / 40 px on the 390 px view. Body text is ProtonSans at 16 px / 24 px. Product content follows in broad beats—private communication, encrypted storage/sharing, defense, meetings, and privacy-respecting AI—before business, ownership, company story, and community sections. The effect is a recognizably editorial serif paired with a service catalog; the service grid provides a visual map before the visitor must choose a product.

Desktop navigation separates Personal, Business, Download, Discover Proton, Resources, and Pricing. Mobile opens into Products, Privacy, Company, and Connect groups; I opened the **Open menu** control and sent Escape. The official Mail detail page restates the privacy promise as “Secure email that protects your privacy” (48 px ProtonSerif) and moves into encryption benefits, import, plans, features, and comparisons. Homepage and detail use a consistent visual system, while the detail page narrows the promise to a single product. The hero stayed visually stable in the timed top-fold comparison. **Transfer:** use a short mission line and a small set of clearly named product capabilities before explaining implementation. **Reject:** Proton’s account-first product-grid model, broad ecosystem breadth, and subscription upsell are not the right shape for a small local archive.

### 2. Signal — plain privacy language, followed by visible reasons

The desktop hero uses a periwinkle field, a large device image, and a left-aligned action. “Speak Freely” measures 60 px / 64 px in Inter 800 on desktop and 40 px / 44 px on mobile; body copy is 16 px / 24 px. The first fold places a descriptive sentence and **Get Signal** beside the device demonstration, then leads into “Why use Signal?” and illustrated feature cards. The page continues with privacy, no-ad/no-tracker, and nonprofit framing in discrete sections. The quiet white sections let the colored hero image carry the visual weight instead of adding texture behind every message.

There is no desktop mega-menu: the header links directly to Get Signal, Help, Blog, Developers, Careers, and Donate. The mobile **menu** opens a simple vertical list with those same responsibilities plus language; the saved open-menu screenshot shows the panel displacing the page at the top, with the device image continuing below. The chosen official detail page is Signal Security Acknowledgments: it provides a vulnerability-reporting route and dated acknowledgments, supporting the privacy promise through a concrete trust destination. No purpose-built motion was visible in the hero during timed captures. First keyboard Tab landed on the Get Signal link with a visible blue 2 px outline. **Transfer:** pair one human benefit with a recognizable real product visual, then offer a small number of evidence destinations. **Reject:** the messaging-device collage and broad feature breadth; neither fits ZeppBridge’s data model.

### 3. Obsidian — lead with the local app and let features be separate pages

Obsidian’s dark charcoal homepage is product-forward. The first fold pairs “Sharpen your thinking.” with “The free and flexible app for your private thoughts,” a purple Windows download button, a secondary platform link, and large screenshots of the actual notes product. The computed headline is 60 px / 60 px, weight 600, system UI, with tight negative tracking; the mobile headline remains fluid at about 50.7 px / 55.8 px. The surrounding app imagery and copy are also dark, while violet is reserved for the primary action and small section accents. Later sections divide the promise into “Spark ideas,” “Sync securely,” and “Publish instantly,” so each capability has an independent explanation instead of relying on one continuous scroll effect.

The desktop header is a compact direct set—Download, Pricing, Sync, Publish, Enterprise—with Community and Account at the opposite end. The mobile navigation reveals the wider link set (including plugins, web clipper, help, blog, changelog, and community); the language control opens a locale list. I opened the language selector on the official Sync page, where the specific claim “All your notes, on all your devices” resolves into security, sync-control, collaboration, and price sections. This destination preserves the homepage’s app-and-flexibility framing while separating paid sync from the downloaded core app. The timed top view was stable. **Transfer:** make the real application and the downloadable path visible early, then give each capability a direct destination. **Reject:** the all-dark, purple visual identity and implication that every feature is local; ZeppBridge should explain its own local-data path precisely.

### 4. Ente — gentle archive imagery, but the animated conveyor needs a motion opt-out

The white homepage uses an image montage above “Safe home for your photos,” dark text, a vivid green phrase/accent, and pill-shaped sign-up/login actions. On mobile the brand condenses the action to **Download** while the photo montage remains the dominant proof. Figtree is used across the page; body text measures 17 px / 25.5 px. A semantic caveat: the prominent hero promise is not the `h1` returned by the DOM inspection; the `h1` elements observed belong to lower rotating feature content such as “Face recognition.” Do not treat the lower feature-heading measurement as the visual hero’s type specification. The navigation is a short text row—Pricing, Blog, About, Download, Help, GitHub, and Sign up—and the mobile toggle reveals the same useful destinations.

The official Features page carries the home promise into distinct explanations for end-to-end encryption, cross-platform support, backup, sharing, import/export, and other product work. That makes the transition from emotional archive promise to operational details easy to follow. I opened and closed the mobile nav. Lower on the homepage, a visible image/text marquee has `animation-name: scroll`, a 35 s infinite duration, and running state; two captures 2.2 s apart differed in 1.289% of RGB channel samples above a 20-level difference threshold, confirming movement rather than just a declared animation. Decorative asset styles also include long loops and 2.5–3.2 s swings. Crucially, the 35 s marquee still reported `running` with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`; the user preference did not stop it in this test. **Transfer:** use warm, calm language and tangible archive imagery sparingly. **Reject:** moving logo/feature conveyor and floating-card motion until ZeppBridge can honor reduced-motion at the actual animated elements. Ente’s first-party [design-system update](https://ente.com/news/) is useful context, not independent design-award recognition.

### 5. Standard Notes — one sentence, two safe next actions, then a readable feature ladder

The white hero centers “Free your mind.” above a succinct description and two equally visible actions, **Download for free** and **Try live demo**. The headline is Lato 900 at 48 px / 62.4 px desktop and 36 px / 46.8 px mobile; the body uses Avenir Next at 18 px / 23.4 px. Pale, low-contrast geometric decoration stays behind the words; a large illustrated vault landscape begins the next section and becomes the visual anchor for the rest of the page. Following sections use paired image/text cards for writing, file storage, and data control, with generous image area and medium-length descriptions.

Desktop navigation is direct Pricing / Features / Help / Go to web app; the mobile header additionally surfaces a download link. There is no visible desktop dropdown or mobile hamburger in the rendered capture. I followed the public Features destination and used browser Back to return home; its opening “Security without compromise” section continues the home story into note types, privacy, themes, and product structure. The top viewport stayed pixel-stable during the sample. First keyboard Tab reached the brand/home link with a browser-visible auto outline. **Transfer:** keep the hero’s main promise short and let a clear demo and download path answer different visitor intents. **Reject:** the strong 3D fantasy-vault illustration as a substitute for ZeppBridge’s real, legible sample-data application; the actual ZeppBridge preview is stronger proof.

### 6. 1Password — polished dark hero with a very large enterprise product map

The first view has a full-width black product field, white centered headline, supporting text, and interface imagery. The headline is unusually light and large—agileSans at 88 px / 96.8 px, weight 300 on desktop, 48 px / 52.8 px on mobile. A cookie dialog obscures part of the desktop/mobile first fold in the saved first-load evidence. The site has a thin announcement strip followed by a clear Business, Personal, Developers, Partners, Resources, Pricing header and high-visibility sales/start actions. Clicking Business opens a four-column menu organized around audience, platform, features, and solutions; the screenshot records the actual open menu, not just links hidden in the DOM. This grouping is useful for a large range of buyer needs, but it becomes a dense directory.

The official Password Manager page reduces the home platform positioning to “Password management for everyone,” then explains personal and team solutions, autofill/sharing, foundation/security, and FAQs. This is a clear audience-to-product handoff. The home top changed only 0.28% of channel samples between timed screenshots; I could not attribute the small difference to a particular animation, so I do not count it as purposeful motion. An independent recognition source does exist, but it concerns earlier product work: [1Password’s 2019 Webby listing](https://winners.webbyawards.com/2019/websites-and-mobile-sites/general-desktop-mobile-sites/web-services-applications/77948/1password) identifies an Honoree in Web Services & Applications; it is not an award for today’s landing page. **Transfer:** use one calm typographic hierarchy and separate different visitor audiences. **Reject:** the broad enterprise mega-menu, cookie-dialog footprint, platform upsell, and oversized headline scale.

### 7. Bitwarden — high clarity in product grouping; first-fold heading hierarchy is a caution

The first screen is a very pale blue/white field with a blue hero statement, short supporting paragraph, two actions, and a large product image beginning below. Inter Variable is the verified family; body text is 16 px with a measured 20.8 px line height. The visual promise reads as one sentence about securing logins across people, agents, and machines. However, the only `h1` reported in the first-fold DOM capture was a 14 px / 21 px, weight-500 “THE MOST TRUSTED PASSWORD MANAGER” kicker. The visually dominant blue statement did not appear as that captured `h1`; treat this as a potential semantic hierarchy problem, not as a desirable heading pattern. The desktop header offers Business, Personal, Pricing, Downloads, Resources, search, free-start, sales, and login actions.

The mobile menu expands into substantial nested Business, Personal, and Resources groups: product destinations, industries, self-hosting, support, and plan links are visible. I opened the mobile menu and inspected these groups. The official Open Source page narrows the trust story to source repositories and how transparency/security are presented, which is a coherent extension of its homepage promise. A public [Bitwarden brand guide](https://github.com/bitwarden/brand) confirms Inter as the brand’s website family and documents its weights; this supports the typography measurement but is first-party brand guidance, not an independent award. The home view stayed pixel-stable in the sample. **Transfer:** link privacy/transparency claims to a real source or support destination. **Reject:** the many nested product/industry paths and unsupported social-proof or rank claims; neither belongs in a small independent utility’s first screen.

### 8. Tailscale — a compact technical promise supported by a deep documentation tree

The homepage uses a warm near-white surface, neutral text, and a centered headline: “The best secure connectivity platform for the AI era.” It measures 64 px / 70.4 px, Inter 500, on desktop and 37.5 px / 41.25 px at 390 px. Supporting text explains identity-based connectivity in network terms, with Start connecting devices and Contact sales directly below. The first-load Ketch privacy-choice banner covers the bottom of the desktop fold and receives keyboard focus before ordinary page content (a visible 2 px outline on its Reject All control). After rejecting optional cookies in the disposable research context, the mobile hamburger opened a grouped menu for Platform, Solutions, Customers, Community, Partnerships, and Pricing, alongside blog/docs/download/sales links. This is a large sitemap with clear audience categories.

The official docs page “What is Tailscale?” continues the promise through a concise definition, technical details, benefits, use cases, and get-started steps. This is a strong home-to-docs route for technically curious visitors. The visible cookie-banner CSS reports a one-time 0.4 s entrance animation; no product-motion sequence was observed and the timed top screenshot was stable. **Transfer:** keep the primary CTA next to a plain-language definition, with a deeper technical route immediately available. **Reject:** the professional-services/sales surface, broad segmentation, and cookie-first interaction; ZeppBridge’s key proof is the running synthetic app and a specific data path, not a corporate network taxonomy.

### 9. Mullvad — identity and privacy education without a feature-led first fold

Mullvad’s dark navy page gives “Privacy is for the people” 80 px / 80 px, weight 600 in Source Sans Pro on desktop; the 390 px view remains about 78 px / 78 px. Open Sans body text is 16 px / 24 px. A yellow start action contrasts with the blue background; the hero places a large screenshot of the VPN app beside copy about a free/open society and data collection. Scrolling moves into VPN and Browser product sections, pricing, and a longer “Understanding VPN & online privacy” article ladder. The site’s distinct voice is the strongest part: it describes the public value behind the tool before listing the interface controls.

The header links directly to VPN, Browser, Pricing, Downloads, and Why privacy matters; the mobile Menu reveals additional help, FAQ, open-source, and server routes. The official Why Mullvad VPN page is the direct explanatory destination reached from the site’s own navigation structure; it deepens the product reason without forcing an account. The homepage stayed stable across timed top captures. The first keyboard Tab lands on Skip to main content with a yellow 2 px outline. **Transfer:** use one human statement and a true application screenshot as the proof, then place educational material after the main action. **Reject:** advocacy-led copy that overwhelms ZeppBridge’s practical task, the oversized mobile headline, and any implication that privacy is absolute without an accurate data-flow explanation.

### 10. Tresorit — security proof works well as a diagram and a real technical destination

Tresorit’s white/pale-blue business homepage puts a product image beside “secure file exchange & collaboration made easy,” a short compliance/security list, and **Try for free** / **Watch a demo** actions. Graphik measures 48 px / 57.6 px at 500 weight on desktop and 34 px / 40.8 px on mobile; body text is 14 px / 22.4 px. A customer-logo strip occupies the next visual beat, followed by secure-collaboration use cases and a section titled “How Tresorit keeps your data secure and private.” The mobile menu opens as a vertical set of Products, Solutions, Resources, and Pricing accordions; the screenshot also shows a cookie prompt at the bottom of the viewport. These menus map neatly to an enterprise product family, though the homepage logo wall and trial CTA are not portable to ZeppBridge.

The official encryption whitepaper is a real detail destination; the heading and page title connect the homepage’s privacy claim to a technical explanation rather than a decorative seal. Clicking that link opened a new tab; I inspected the page title and heading, closed the tab, and confirmed the homepage remained open. A 0.3 s CSS fade-in is attached to testimonial/video elements in the DOM, but the homepage’s timed top captures did not show measurable motion. The mobile Products/Solutions/Resources/Pricing menu was opened; the testimonial slider controls were present in the page inventory but no state change was verified, so this report does not treat them as an interaction finding. **Transfer:** make a data-security diagram lead to a readable technical source, and keep a demo destination explicit. **Reject:** enterprise-client logo clusters, customer claims, and subscription-trial framing without equivalent ZeppBridge evidence or intent.

## Strongest three references for this page

1. **Obsidian** — closest content-model fit: it puts a real downloadable desktop app in the first view, names a few distinct jobs, and lets a visitor reach a dedicated Sync explanation directly. Borrow the path and section responsibility, not its dark surface or paid sync model.
2. **Signal** — strongest trust communication: a short human benefit is paired with a real app visual, while a simple help/security route can answer deeper questions. Borrow the single-message hierarchy and trust destination; keep ZeppBridge’s statements bounded to the actual local archive, callback/update/error-reporting services, and user-directed export.
3. **Ente** — closest privacy-archive presentation: the homepage feels like a place that holds memories, and the Features page maps the emotional promise into distinct tasks. Borrow the calm descriptive writing and detail-page ladder; reject its moving marquee because the reduced-motion emulation did not stop it.

## Shared principles, transfers, and rejection list

- Lead with one concrete personal result, then explain the privacy mechanism in a nearby sentence. Signal’s everyday promise and Obsidian’s flexible app language are easier to scan than a list of abstract encryption adjectives.
- Let the real product serve as proof. Obsidian, Signal, and Tresorit show their product before a visitor reaches the deeper page; ZeppBridge’s real synthetic sample app is more convincing than a fabricated dashboard or lifestyle image.
- Separate actions by intent and label the destination. Download, view the sample, open the source, and read the connection guide answer different questions; avoid funnels that make the visitor discover that difference after clicking.
- Create a trustworthy trust path: one privacy summary should lead to a real data-flow explanation, missing-sample policy, and docs. The official docs/help destinations on Tailscale, Mullvad, and Tresorit show how technical depth can live one click away.
- Keep navigation sized to the task. Small direct-link headers (Signal, Standard Notes, Obsidian) are more compatible with the five ZeppBridge chapters than Bitwarden’s business/personal/industry mega-menu or 1Password’s four-column catalog.
- Preserve the warm-paper/olive/charcoal direction and MiSans. Take the measured editorial hierarchy: compact kicker, readable statement, short body measure, clear actions, and section titles that remain understandable without motion.
- Make every motion optional. Ente’s 35-second marquee is visibly moving and did not pause under reduced-motion emulation; that is a concrete pattern to avoid. Prefer a brief state transition that pauses offscreen and has an immediate static/reduced-motion state.
- Use precise privacy language. Cloud privacy sites can explain encryption and offer proof pages, but ZeppBridge should say local archive first, state what is actually transmitted for OAuth/update/optional error reports, and name the external destination only when a user exports a file to an AI tool.

**Reject for ZeppBridge:** unsupported usage/rating/customer numbers; corporate logo walls; dark/light palette copying; login or account-first CTA; multiple audiences and business taxonomy; absolute claims that the whole project has no server; hidden scroll-controlled copy; unattended loops, scrolling marquees, sticky page takeover, or demo actions that are not truly reversible. Keep stable download, the in-development v3 synthetic demo, source, and operating-system availability distinct.

## Accessibility and evidence limits

Keyboard first-Tab spot checks surfaced visible focus on Signal (blue 2 px), Mullvad (yellow 2 px skip link), Tailscale’s privacy-choice control (2 px), and browser-auto outlines on Proton, Obsidian, Ente, Standard Notes, Bitwarden, and Tresorit. On the captured 1Password state, the first Tab target was an empty `DIV` at the bottom edge, so that spot check did not establish a visible focus indicator. These are single-start-state checks, not full keyboard audits. The Ente reduced-motion result is the only motion preference I measured against a specific moving element; do not generalize it to other sites. No frame-time/performance trace was collected. Cookie banners and current web content can vary with geography and date; the screenshots preserve what the anonymous `en-US` session actually showed on 2026-10-07.

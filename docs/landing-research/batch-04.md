## Cross-site principles

1. Make the first sentence answer one real user question, and pair it with one primary action that directly opens the product, demo or download.
2. A product screenshot or controlled sample can explain more than several generic feature claims. Keep every displayed state authentic and visibly connected to the actual product.
3. Use a small number of task labels to organize later detail pages. A navigation menu should reduce choice, not become a second product catalogue.
4. Separate the homepage’s short orientation from deeper feature/use-case pages; send a visitor to detail when they want specifics, without making the homepage a list of everything.
5. Let typography and color support the product’s character. A health archive benefits from legible numbers, restrained warmth and stable visual hierarchy, not AI novelty effects.
6. Treat motion and large assets as costs. Several pages use loops or hero video; a real, readable sample still can preserve meaning with less load and fewer distractions.
7. Keep privacy, ownership and data completeness specific. A product preview should show source and missing-value states clearly; never adapt “complete every gap” language to imply health values were measured when they were not.

## Three strongest references for ZeppBridge

- **Cursor — real software surface at the point of decision.** Transfer the large, believable product window beside a direct download CTA. For ZeppBridge, show the existing sample-data app and a recognizable archive task; do not create simulated health records or fictional activity.
- **Hugging Face — direct routes into data.** Transfer a visible search field and a few short, stable categories only if they map to true archive destinations (for example overview, activity and measurements). Keep resources/help distinct from data categories and avoid a platform-scale mega menu.
- **Meshy — concise mode choice.** Transfer the small three-option selector only if each selection updates the real sample view and clearly labels its purpose. A ZeppBridge sample might switch between summary, record detail and export preview while keeping its existing five-scroll-beat explanation intact.

## Patterns to reject

- Broad “AI assistant / does everything” positioning, absolute “#1” claims, unverified outcome numbers or endorsement-led proof. They weaken a precise local-first health-data promise.
- Megamenus spanning tools, teams, marketplaces, resources, community and enterprise when visitors need only to understand import, review and export.
- Autoplay-heavy illustration, persistent looping decoration or a consent panel that covers the core demo. Prefer user-controlled motion and let the first screen breathe.
- A required account or sales conversation as the only route to understand the product. ZeppBridge’s sample and local download should be useful before account creation, and no account should be implied as necessary.

## Concrete transfer suggestions

1. Keep the first screen focused on local ownership and a single “Explore the sample archive” action beside the already-real interactive sample app; place the download as a clear second action.
2. Within the sample, use real data and explicit `null`/missing states. Explain how to connect/import, where values come from and how to export close to the corresponding view.
3. Group the lower page by user task—connect a device, inspect records, export/share, optional deliberate AI handoff—rather than by implementation subsystem or a long generic feature grid.
4. Make data-flow/privacy and supported connection methods scannable, link to their focused details, and reserve motion for deliberate interactions. Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep all actions keyboard named.

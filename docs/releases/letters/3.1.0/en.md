Hello, I'm Lingcang, the developer of ZeppBridge. As you can see, this is a letter, just one that arrives on your computer screen instead of in your mailbox. If all goes to plan, every update from now on will come with a letter like this one.

I'm doing this because I want to hear from you more easily. Most people in China find ZeppBridge through Bilibili or Xiaohongshu, and most people elsewhere find it through Reddit or GitHub. But reporting a problem in any of those places is slow and awkward. If I put myself in your shoes and didn't have a GitHub account, I probably wouldn't bother opening an issue either. So I kept wondering whether there was a more direct way, something you could do the moment you notice a problem. That's how these letters came about.

Starting with this one, you can write back at the end of the letter. Whatever you think of ZeppBridge, any suggestion or idea you have, just write it down. Your reply reaches me anonymously, and I read every single one; unless you tick “Allow anonymous quoting”, I won't share your words. Your draft saves itself as you type, and if you're offline, it waits on your computer and goes out once you're back online.

The letter box has a few other nice things in it. Each week you'll get a letter about your data, sent as soon as the week is over, with a short summary of how your body did. You can also write to your future self, six months, a year or three years ahead. I can't read those letters; they stay on your own computer and only reach you on the day you picked.

Now about 3.1.0 itself. Yes, I skipped versions again, straight from 3.0.6 to 3.1.0, because this update really is a big one. The biggest piece is the new Journal page. It has a calendar of your whole year, one small square per day, a bit like the contribution graph on GitHub. You can record injuries by tapping the right spot on a body drawn like a page from Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks. You can name your running shoes and track their mileage, and it will remind you when a pair is due to retire. There's a PB wall too, with your best 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon times, worked out straight from the workout data on your computer. And you can add your races, with a countdown and a priority, then claim your result with one click afterwards.

My favourite part is the Rings. Your workout history becomes the cross-section of a real tree, one ring for each year, and a ring swells a little in the weeks you trained more. I've always liked comparing people to trees. Your rings quietly keep every road you ran, every hill you climbed and every session you put in, and you can turn them into an image to share with one click.

Training with AI has also become a little smoother. After you finish a workout plan you sent to your watch, ZeppBridge will ask how it went; if it felt too hard, you can make the next session 10% easier with one click, and it only asks once per workout, so it won't keep nagging you. You can now save the prompts you send to AI as tasks, keep the earlier wordings so you can bring them back any time, and export them to share with others. Along the way, I also reworked a lot of the animations so the app feels more alive.

Finally, getting data in and out. You can now import data exported from Strava and Apple Health, as well as GPX and FIT files, into ZeppBridge; and everything you've written and organised here can be exported as a single JSON file to take with you. I want ZeppBridge to be a bridge in the literal sense: its only job is to connect your data, never to lock you in. You can bring your data in from anywhere and take any of it back out, and all of it is open, free and shared.

There's much more in this update that I'll leave for you to find on your own, and the next page lists every change in detail. It wasn't until I sat down to write this letter that I realised how much had piled up. I may make it sound easy, but getting here took quite a lot of back and forth, which is why it's a week later than planned. Sorry about that.

Winter is on its way, at least where I am, and my runs are getting colder. Wrap up warm out there.

Wishing you good health and all the best. See you in the next version!

— Lingcang

- Added: **Start at login**. Turn it on in Settings › Sync and updates and ZeppBridge opens when you sign in to your computer and syncs as usual; you can also have it stay in the tray without opening a window.
- Added: **A question of the day on the home page**. The home page now holds a single card with the one question most worth asking your AI today: review the race you just ran, check whether a fresh PR means you are getting faster, look back at a workout you just finished, a night your recovery was off, a rough night or a long break, and on ordinary days plan tomorrow. Not the question you want? Tap the refresh button on the left for another one, as many times as you like. Open the card and the matching workouts and nights are dealt into a hand right there: swap some cards from the reserve, then copy it straight to the AI you picked, or fine-tune it in Send to AI; when you close it the cards fold back into the card. You can hide it for today, or turn it off in Settings › AI.
- Added: **Day, month and year views for the workout and sleep lists**. Hold Ctrl and scroll, or pinch on a touchpad, to move one level at a time; drag the tray at the bottom left to do the same, and open it for selecting records and importing workouts. Each month opens with a summary: average sleep, score, bedtime and the change from the month before, or workouts, distance and time. The sleep year view adds a year analysis above the heatmap, and the workout year view is the whole journal. Sleep rows show duration, score and dots for falling asleep and waking up; workout rows show a small route outline (skipped under 1 km). The old Recent records page is folded into these two lists, and long lists keep the years marked beside the scrollbar.
- Added: **Attach a workout structure sample to a letter to the author**. For a workout the app cannot read yet, such as a pool swim's strokes per length, tap Sample on the letter to attach your most recent workout, or use Write to the author on a workout's source card to attach that one; you can open the sample and read all of it before sending. It keeps only field names, structure and codes such as stroke, pool length, distance and duration. Heart rate and other health values become placeholders, coordinates, places, device and account IDs are removed, and times are shifted to the year 2000.
- Improved: **Heart rate zones in more places**. Medium and small heart rate cards in workout details are now colored by zone, with a thin zone bar underneath; point at it to see each zone’s share and time. The training status page adds weekly zone-time bars that say which zone model they use.
- Improved: **Confirmations stay inside the app**. Deleting, disconnecting or signing out no longer opens a system dialog but a glass panel that matches the interface; irreversible actions are marked in red, and Esc cancels.
- Improved: **One more hint when a sync succeeds but finds nothing**. You may have signed in to a different account than the one your watch is paired with: if that account was created with Google or Apple, add an email to it in the Zepp app first, then sign in with that email.
- Improved: **The light theme is a neutral warm grey**. Backgrounds, text and outlines lose the faint green tint they had.
- Improved: **A redrawn Zepp Cloud icon**. The Zepp Cloud icon in the home page’s data sources is now a vector glass cloud that stays sharp on high-resolution screens.
- Improved: **An easier-to-read sleep stage chart**. Stages now sit on four labeled rows (Awake, REM, Light, Deep); the time axis and the start and end times show AM and PM, so a night that starts before midnight and ends in the morning reads at a glance (a 24-hour setting is still respected); point at a stretch and it hops up with its length and times; the time and share of each stage moved above the chart and double as its legend; "Source and device" starts folded.
- Fixed: **Moving from a beta to the matching release now delivers that release’s letter from the author**. Before, only the release notes appeared and the letter was treated as already read.
- Fixed: **Life event names no longer pile up on trend charts**. When several eras sit close together, their names go on two lines, each on its own band; any that still do not fit are left off the chart and still show when you hover.
